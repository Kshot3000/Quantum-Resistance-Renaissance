QTC Pay Desk

Accept Quantus like a merchant: build invoices with exact planck amounts, hand the customer a QR payment request, run a point-of-sale screen, keep a register, and embed a tip button on your site. This desk never holds keys or funds — it only prepares and records payment requests. You confirm payment in your own wallet or an explorer, then mark it paid here.

Read this before you take anyone's QTC

  • No custody, no settlement guarantee. Pay Desk creates payment requests and records what you report. A transaction only exists when you see it in your wallet or the chain explorer.
  • Marking "paid" is your attestation, not chain truth. Always verify the tx yourself — search the amount and address in the Block Explorer — before you release goods or mark an invoice paid.
  • There is no listed spot price yet. QTC's first announced venue is NEAR Intents; no listing date was confirmed as of 2026-10-01. The rate card below is a manual entry — set it from whatever price you and the customer agree on, and stamp the timestamp.
  • Amounts are exact to the planck. QTC has 12 decimals (1 QTC = 1012 plancks, verified from on-chain block rewards). QR codes encode your address; the amount travels alongside as copyable text — there is no QTC payment-URI standard yet, so the QR never invents one.

Confirmation guidance

Quantus is proof-of-work: finality is probabilistic, not instant. Blocks land when they land (the Sept 30 chain snapshot averaged ~14 s between blocks; week-one mainnet was faster — times move with difficulty). Suggested confirmation counts by invoice value are merchant heuristics, not protocol guarantees.

< 1 QTC2 confirmations

Coffee-money. Fine for walk-up counter sales.

1 – 50 QTC6 confirmations

Typical retail. ~1–2 minutes at current pace.

50 – 500 QTC12 confirmations

High-ticket. Wait a few minutes before releasing goods.

500+ QTC24+ confirmations

Treasury-scale. Consider escrow or staged delivery.

  • Watch the address, not just the amount. Confirm the receiving address in the explorer/wallet matches your invoice's address exactly — address poisoning is cheap and quiet. The Contact Vault desk explains the attack.
  • Small amounts stay practical. On-chain fees on Quantus are low by design; still, don't accept dust below the 0.001 QTC existential deposit — an invoice total that can't keep an account alive is an accounting headache.
  • Expiry is yours to enforce. If the rate moves before payment arrives, the expired request is void — generate a fresh invoice at the fresh rate.

Merchant playbook

The operational habits that separate a smooth QTC checkout from a support ticket.

  • One invoice, one receiving address. Reusing a single address across customers leaks your whole revenue history and invites poisoning. Generate a fresh address per invoice in your wallet (the Web Wallet derives addresses from your seed; Address Toolkit inspects them).
  • Price in fiat, settle in QTC. Quote USD, convert at your stamped rate, lock the QTC amount on the invoice. Crypto-denominated pricing transfers volatility to the customer.
  • Decide refunds before you need them. Publish your policy: refunds in QTC at the original invoice rate (not current) is the standard honest answer. Get a return address up front for large orders.
  • Keep your keys off the counter. The device that builds invoices should be view-capable only. Signing keys live in the Web Wallet or Airgap Desk — never in the register, never in the browser tab the customer sees.
  • Taxes are your accountant's job, not this page's. The Ledger Desk keeps a miner/holder tax ledger; export this register's CSV into it for your books. Nothing here is tax advice.
  • Have an outage story. If your wallet or the explorer is unreachable, fall back to: invoice paid → customer shows you the tx in their wallet → you confirm the sending address and amount → goods on trust, settled later. Write it down before Friday night.

Limits: Pay Desk cannot watch the chain, cannot convert to fiat, cannot hold funds, carries no price oracle, and cannot guarantee a payment.
