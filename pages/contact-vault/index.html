🎓How address poisoning works

1 · Dust from a doppelgänger

On account-based chains like Quantus, anyone can send a tiny transaction from any address they control. An attacker grinds a keypair until the address shares the first and last few characters with yours, then sends your wallet dust. Their lookalike now sits in your transaction history — and when you next copy "your" address from history instead of your vault, you copy theirs.

2 · Clipboard hijackers

Malware watches the clipboard for anything shaped like an address and silently swaps in the attacker's lookalike between your copy and your paste. You pasted qz… — but is it the qz… you copied?

3 · Why five words beat two eyes

Humans check the start and end of an address and skim the middle — exactly the parts an attacker keeps identical. A checkphrase is different: it is a 55-bit checksum, so any change anywhere in the address avalanches into completely different words. There are 255 — about 36 quadrillion — possible five-word combinations.

"Each of the words has 2048 (2^11) choices so if we choose 5 words, we have a total set of 2^55 ~ 36 quadrillion possible combinations… With PBKDF2 (40,000 iterations)… a rainbow table would take 2^55 * 14ms ≈ 16 million years. A high-end GPU… Even then: 2^55 * 1.4ms ≈ 1.6 million years per GPU. With a cluster of 1,000 GPUs working in parallel: ~1,600 years." — Quantus-Network/qp-human-checkphrase README, Security Analysis (quoted verbatim)

Be honest with yourself: attackers can grind similar-looking prefixes — grinding is cheap for a few characters. That is exactly why you verify words, not prefixes. A matching prefix proves nothing; matching words prove the exact address.

📜Honest scope

What this desk can and cannot do for you — no fine print anywhere else.

  • DOES — detect swapped or mistyped addressesA checkphrase is a checksum rendered as words. If the address you pasted isn't byte-identical to the one you saved, the words change. That is the whole alarm.
  • DOES NOT — prove who owns an addressA checkphrase is public: anyone can compute the five words for any address — that is the point. Matching words prove the address is the one you saved, not that the label you gave it is true. Verify ownership out-of-band.
  • DOES — keep your vault on this machineContacts live in this browser's localStorage. No account, no sync, no server ever sees them. Clear site data and the vault is gone — keep an encrypted backup.
  • DOES NOT — secure your keysThis tool never asks for, sees, or touches private keys or seed phrases. If any page claiming to be this desk asks for them, it is a scam.
  • DOES NOT — make you invincibleMalware that can read your screen can read the words too. The desk raises the cost of fooling you enormously; pair it with a clean machine and out-of-band confirmation for large transfers.
