QTC Pool Desk

Every known Quantus mining pool, compared honestly: real fees stacked the way they actually hit you, payout terms, PPLNS windows, and copy-paste connection commands — each fact stamped with the pool page it came from. Pools change their terms; re-verify on the pool's own site before you connect.

Read this before you point a miner anywhere

  • Pools are counterparties, not the chain. Until a payout lands in your wallet, you are trusting the operator's accounting. Both pools below say so themselves in different words.
  • Address only. A pool needs your qz… payout address — nothing else. Never paste a 24-word phrase into pool software or a pool website.
  • Re-copy endpoints and TLS pins from the pool's own site. Servers and certificate pins rotate; a stale pin fails permanently. Anything on this page is a snapshot dated 2026-10-01.
  • One GPU, one miner. Never run a pool miner and your own node (or two pool miners) on the same card — rewards follow hashrate, not worker count.

Pool vs Solo vs your own node

The real tradeoff: a pool sells you variance smoothing. Solo keeps the whole block but pays you in lottery tickets.

How PPLNS actually works

Both pools pay PPLNS — Pay Per Last N Shares.

1. You submit shares

Your miner proves work continuously. Valid shares enter the pool's window; stale or invalid ones don't.

2. The pool finds a block

The block reward (minus the pool fee) is split across every share inside the window — weighted by share difficulty.

3. The window slides

AriaPool's window is 2 × the block's difficulty — roughly two blocks' worth of network work. Shares older than that fall out. Stop mining and your weight decays to zero: pool-hopping across a dry spell earns you nothing.

4. Confirmations, then payout

Rewards credit once the block is confirmed (Quanpool: 105 blocks). Then the payout schedule runs — twice daily on AriaPool, hourly on Quanpool — and balances below the minimum carry over.

Luck cuts both ways: a pool that finds blocks faster than expected pays above the statistical average for a while, and vice versa. The comparator above shows the expectation; the chain decides the reality.

Run your own pool

For operators: not-only-mining-pool is open-source Go pool software with a Quantus engine in the default build.

  • Speaks both miner dialects — native QUIC (quantus-miner/2 with token auth + certificate pinning) and LuckyPool Quantus Stratum over TCP/TLS, so the official miner and SRBMiner-Multi work concurrently.
  • Pure-Go Poseidon2 verification of 512-bit nonces with strict U512 targets; fixed integer share difficulty per port.
  • PROP/SOLO payouts driven by finalized chain events with an independently funded signing wallet.
  • Validated end-to-end on a real node in CI — per the repo's engine docs, every engine below the reference Bitcoin path is E2E-tested against a live node.

Source: github.com/mining-pool/not-only-mining-pool (README + docs/PLUGGABLE_ENGINES.md), verified 2026-10-01. Running a pool makes you a custodian of other people's hashrate — the trust section at the top applies to you doubly.

Safety checklist

  • ✓ Pool gets your qz… address. Nothing else. Ever.
  • ✓ Verify miner SHA-256 checksums before running anything you downloaded.
  • ✓ Re-copy the server address and TLS pin from the pool's live page — never from a screenshot, a chat message, or this desk.
  • ✓ Watch your address on the pool's own stats page after the first share; if it never appears, your endpoint or pin is wrong.
  • ✓ Keep miner/RPC/metrics ports off the public internet; only P2P (30333/tcp) should be public on a node.
  • ✗ Never paste your 24-word phrase into pool software or a website. Address only.
  • ✗ Don't "verify" a pool by sending it QTC — payouts flow pool → you, never the reverse.
